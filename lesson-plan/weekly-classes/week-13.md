# Week 13: Submission Phases

## Class Goal

Students apply all five submission phases in order, then finish with control and mechanical efficiency.

## Concepts

1. Five submission phases.
2. Mechanical efficiency.

## Memory Phrase

Control earns submissions.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: do not finish until all five phases are complete.
2. Phase 1, isolate: trap target limb or neck line so it cannot freely escape.
3. Phase 2, expose: create angle and opening that makes the finish available.
4. Phase 3, break defense: remove partner's defensive frames and hand fighting.
5. Phase 4, maintain control: keep dominant position and anchor points through the transition.
6. Phase 5, finish: apply controlled mechanics with alignment, not speed-only force.
7. Common fail point: students skip Phase 3 and rush to finishing mechanics.
8. Recovery rule: if finish stalls, identify missing phase and return to that phase first.
9. Transition from Week 12: Week 12 taught style switching during passing; Week 13 applies the same decision discipline to submission sequencing.

Why?

1. Skipping defense-breaking means the opponent still has active tools to escape. The finish becomes a strength contest instead of a mechanical advantage.
2. Position loss during a failed finish resets you to neutral or worse, wasting all prior control work.
3. Phased submissions give you multiple decision points to confirm control before committing.
4. Mechanical efficiency means less energy spent per finish, which matters across multiple rounds or longer matches.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: The finish is the final step, not the first step.
Teaching point: Isolate, expose, break defense, maintain control, then finish.

### Core Script

First concept is five-phase submission sequencing. You only move forward when the current phase is complete. If a phase breaks, you go back one step and rebuild.

Second concept is mechanical efficiency. Efficient finishes come from alignment, angle, and body connection, not isolated arm squeezing.

Watch this rep from mount: isolate the arm, expose angle, break defensive grip, maintain chest and hip control, then finish with hip-driven mechanics. I do not jump to finish before defense is broken.

Common mistake: student isolates then rushes finish while defense is still active. Result: partner escapes and top loses control. Correction: return to break-defense phase, then finish.

## Drill

Drill 17: Five-Phase Submission Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start from mount or back control.
2. Run all five phases in order, isolate, expose, break defense, maintain control, finish.
3. Complete each phase before progressing to the next.
4. Partner gives realistic defense at Phase 3 so attacker must actively break defense.
5. Finish only after partner confirms control was maintained.
6. If sequence breaks, reset to the first missing phase and continue.

## Drill 2

Finish Mechanics Isolation.

Setup:

1. Start with submission already at Phase 4 (defense broken, control maintained). Partner sets up the position cooperatively.
2. Attacker focuses only on the Phase 5 finish using body alignment, hip drive, and connected mechanics.
3. No arm squeezing or speed-based force.
4. Partner gives feedback: "efficient" or "muscle" after each rep.
5. Reset and repeat until 3 consecutive efficient finishes. Run from both armbar and cross-collar choke setups.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Passing to submission chains improve when phases are respected.
2. Sweep finishes also require exposure and control before completion.
3. Defense reading improves when you know which phase is missing.
4. Takedown finishes also improve when control and exposure are completed before forceful finish attempts.
