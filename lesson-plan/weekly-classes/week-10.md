# Week 10: Create Reactions, Then Choose

## Class Goal

Students create predictable reactions, then choose the correct sweep or submission follow-up.

## Concepts

1. Off-balancing.
2. Sweep versus submission decision.

## Memory Phrase

Create the reaction, take what opens.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: force a reaction first, then choose the opening the reaction creates.
2. Off-balancing: controlled kuzushi that shifts posture, base, or weight before attack commitment.
3. Sweep-versus-submission decision: choose sweep when posture/base breaks, choose submission when limb/neck exposure appears.
4. Sequence order: create reaction, read response, choose follow-up, then finish with control.
5. Common fail point: students pre-decide second attack before reading partner reaction.
6. Control target: keep one stabilizing connection while switching attack categories.
7. Recovery rule: if reaction is unclear or control breaks, reset to first threat and re-create reaction.
8. Transition from Week 9: Week 9 rebuilt guard to Layer 1, Week 10 converts stable guard into decision-based offense.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Decision quality beats forcing a predetermined plan.
Teaching point: Use the first threat to force reaction, then choose the second opening.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we built layered guard retention to recover safely. This week we turn recovery into offense through off-balancing and better choices.

Today's focus is reaction-based offense. You are not chaining attacks randomly. You are creating a reaction, reading it, and choosing what opens.
Repeat with me: create the reaction, take what opens. In every rep today, the second action must match your partner's actual response.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is off-balancing. Off-balance does not mean throwing someone. It means moving their base enough that they must post, pull back, or change posture.

Second concept is sweep-versus-submission decision making. If your partner posts and widens base, sweep lane often opens. If they hide base but expose arm or neck, submission lane often opens.

Watch this rep: I threaten a sweep from butterfly. Partner posts hard to stop it. I keep connection and switch to submission entry on the exposed arm. I do not switch early before the reaction appears.

Common mistake: student alternates attacks on a timer instead of reading reactions. Result: second attack arrives with no opening and fails. Correction: pause one beat, read posture and posts, then choose.

Partner cue for this block: call out "reaction first" before every second attack.

## Drill

Drill 14: Reaction-Choice Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in closed or butterfly guard.
2. Initiate first threat to force a real defensive reaction.
3. Read the reaction: post/base response or exposure response.
4. Choose second action based on that reaction, sweep or submission.
5. Finish with control for a 2-count before resetting.
6. If second action is not reaction-matched, reset and replay with slower read.

Coaching cues:

1. Require clear first threat before any switch.
2. Reward correct read-and-choose behavior before finish speed.
3. Require verbal callout each rep: "reaction first."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in closed guard or butterfly guard.
2. Goal: alternate threats based on reactions.
3. Round 1 constraint: second attack only counts if reaction is identified out loud.
4. Round 2 progression: no verbal callout, but coach checks if second action matched reaction.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained reaction-based decisions, not pre-planned chains.
You created a first threat, read what your partner gave you, and chose the correct second action.
In live rounds this week, score clean read-and-choose chains before submission counts.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Top control: threaten one side to expose opposite side.
2. Passing: fake pressure to open movement pass lanes.
3. Submission entries: first threat opens second finish line.
4. Stand-up exchanges: off-balance reactions create takedown entry windows.

## Win Condition

Student consistently chooses second attack based on partner's real defensive reaction.

## Metric

Successful reaction-matched sweep/submission chains per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean reaction-choice chains per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: percentage of second attacks that correctly match the observed reaction.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

3. Use reaction to choose the second action.
4. Track successful two-step alternations.
5. Add one quality rule per round: second action only counts if you can name the reaction that triggered it.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Repeats same attack despite clear defense.
   Quick correction: Switch threat category on next attempt.
2. Mistake: Off-balance attempt without angle change.
   Quick correction: Create angle first, then attack.
3. Mistake: Rushes finish after first opening.
   Quick correction: Confirm control before committing.
4. Mistake: Student chooses second attack before reading reaction.
   Quick correction: Pause one beat, identify posture/base reaction, then choose.
