# Week 14: Integrate The Full System

## Class Goal

Students integrate dilemmas, chaining, pacing, and transitions into one repeatable system under resistance.

## Concepts

1. Dilemma creation and chaining.
2. Pacing and energy management.

## Memory Phrase

Present two threats, then flow.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: create two real threats, read response, then chain immediately.
2. Dilemma creation: present two credible options that both create consequence if ignored.
3. Chaining: predetermined next link that activates when opponent chooses one defense.
4. Pacing and energy management: change tempo deliberately, not emotionally.
5. Sequence order: establish control, present dilemma, read defense, chain, stabilize, then continue.
6. Common fail point: students present fake second threat and stall after first defense.
7. Recovery rule: if chain breaks, return to last stable control and rebuild the dilemma.
8. Transition from Week 13: Week 13 organized finishing phases, Week 14 integrates all weekly skills into one decision loop.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Advanced control is giving opponents two bad options.
Teaching point: Build two real threats, chain immediately, and manage pace deliberately.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we treated submission as a process. This week we integrate all concepts into one connected system.

Today's focus is integration under pressure. You are not hunting random moves. You are running a decision loop with structure.
Repeat with me: present two threats, then flow. In every rep today, if your chain stalls, return to structure and restart the loop.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is dilemma creation and chaining. A dilemma is only real if both threats are credible and connected to your control.

Second concept is pacing and energy management. Fast pace is useful only when timed with reaction windows. Slow pace is useful only when building control.

Watch this rep: from stable control, I present Threat A and Threat B. Partner defends Threat A. I chain instantly to Threat B, stabilize, then continue sequence at controlled pace.

Common mistake: student pauses after first defense and loses pressure. Result: opponent resets position. Correction: pre-select your next link before initiating first threat.

Partner cue for this block: call out "A, B, chain" before each sequence.

## Drill

Drill 18: Full-System Integration Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Choose one starting position, guard, passing, or top control.
2. Establish stable control before first threat.
3. Present Threat A and Threat B with clear intent.
4. Read defense and chain immediately to the correct link.
5. Stabilize new position for a 2-count before next chain.
6. If chain breaks, return to last stable control and rebuild.

Coaching cues:

1. Require credible Threat A and Threat B, not fake gestures.
2. Reward immediate, reaction-matched chaining before speed.
3. Require verbal callout each rep: "A, B, chain, stabilize."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in neutral or chosen control position.
2. Goal: present two threats and chain to next action.
3. Round 1 constraint: chain only counts if second threat is reaction-matched.
4. Round 2 progression: add pace declaration, slow-build or fast-chain, and maintain it for full round.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we integrated the full system under pressure: structure, dilemma, chain, and pace.
When your threats were credible and your next link was ready, your flow stayed stable.
In live rounds this week, score clean two-threat chains with stabilization before counting finishes.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Dilemma logic applies in guard, passing, and submission phases.
2. Pacing choices apply in both attack and defense transitions.
3. When chain fails, return to structure and restart decision loop.
4. Competition rounds: planned pace cycles reduce panic and improve late-round decisions.

## Win Condition

Student creates a real two-threat dilemma and chains to the correct next action under resistance.

## Metric

Successful reaction-matched two-threat chains per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean integrated chains per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: percentage of chains that include a stabilization hold before next action.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Two-Threat Chain.
2. In live rounds, present Threat A and Threat B from one position.
3. When opponent defends one, immediately switch to the other.
4. Track completed two-link chains.
5. Add one quality rule per round: no chain counts unless you stabilize for a 2-count after the switch.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Threats are not credible so opponent ignores one.
   Quick correction: Build better control before dilemma.
2. Mistake: Pauses after first defense.
   Quick correction: Predetermine next link before starting.
3. Mistake: Pace is random and energy drains early.
   Quick correction: Declare pace objective before round starts.
4. Mistake: Student chains without stabilizing and loses position on next exchange.
   Quick correction: Require 2-count stabilization before continuing chain.
