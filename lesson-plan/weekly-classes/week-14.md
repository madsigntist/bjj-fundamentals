# Week 14: Dilemma Creation And Chaining

## Class Goal

Students integrate dilemmas, chaining, pacing, and transitions into one repeatable system under resistance.

## Concepts

1. Dilemma creation and chaining.
2. Pacing and energy management.

## Memory Phrase

Present two threats, then flow.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: create two real threats, read response, then chain immediately.
2. Dilemma creation: present two credible options that both create consequence if ignored.
3. Chaining: predetermined next link that activates when opponent chooses one defense.
4. Pacing and energy management: change tempo deliberately, not emotionally.
5. Sequence order: establish control, present dilemma, read defense, chain, stabilize, then continue.
6. Common fail point: students present fake second threat and stall after first defense.
7. Recovery rule: if chain breaks, return to last stable control and rebuild the dilemma.
8. Transition from Week 13: Week 13 organized finishing phases, Week 14 integrates all weekly skills into one decision loop.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Advanced control is giving opponents two bad options.
Teaching point: Build two real threats, chain immediately, and manage pace deliberately.

### Core Script

First concept is dilemma creation and chaining. A dilemma is only real if both threats are credible and connected to your control.

Second concept is pacing and energy management. Fast pace is useful only when timed with reaction windows. Slow pace is useful only when building control.

Watch this rep: from stable control, I present Threat A and Threat B. Partner defends Threat A. I chain instantly to Threat B, stabilize, then continue sequence at controlled pace.

Common mistake: student pauses after first defense and loses pressure. Result: opponent resets position. Correction: pre-select your next link before initiating first threat.

## Drill

Drill 18: Full-System Integration Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Choose one starting position, guard, passing, or top control.
2. Establish stable control before first threat.
3. Present Threat A and Threat B with clear intent.
4. Read defense and chain immediately to the correct link.
5. Stabilize new position for a 2-count before next chain.
6. If chain breaks, return to last stable control and rebuild.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Dilemma logic applies in guard, passing, and submission phases.
2. Pacing choices apply in both attack and defense transitions.
3. When chain fails, return to structure and restart decision loop.
4. Competition rounds: planned pace cycles reduce panic and improve late-round decisions.
