# Week 6: Head And Hips Steer Everything

## Class Goal

Students maintain dual head and hip control before attacking.

## Concepts

1. Head control.
2. Hip control.

## Memory Phrase

Head and hips steer everything.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: control head and hips before attacking.
2. Head control: align and steer your partner's head so they cannot freely turn toward you.
3. Hip control: block or pin hip movement so they cannot freely recover guard or build base.
4. Sequence order: establish head control, connect hip block, settle pressure, then advance.
5. Common fail point: chasing submissions after controlling only one anchor.
6. Control target: keep at least one head anchor and one hip anchor throughout transitions.
7. Recovery rule: if either anchor is lost, pause advancement and re-anchor first.
8. Transition from Week 5: inside position wins entry, head and hip control wins stabilization.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: One control point is good. Two control points are dominant.
Teaching point: If either head or hips are free, expect escapes. Re-anchor both before attacking.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we focused on inside position and hand fighting to win the first battle. This week we sharpen control by managing the two anchors: head and hips.

Today's focus is what happens after you win inside position. Inside gets you in. Head and hip control keep you in control.
Repeat with me: head and hips steer everything. In every rep today, control both anchors before trying to advance or submit.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is head control. In top side control, your shoulder pressure and chest angle should steer the head so the bottom player cannot turn in freely.

Second concept is hip control. Your near-side knee, hip block, or elbow-knee connection on top should prevent the bottom player from recovering knee line and rebuilding guard.

Watch this rep: I establish head control first, then connect hip control, then settle pressure for two seconds. Only after both anchors are stable do I transition or attack.

Common mistake: student crossfaces hard but leaves hips free. Result: bottom player hip-escapes and recovers guard. Correction: re-anchor hip block before chasing the next move.

Partner cue for this block: call out "head, hips, then move" on every rep.

## Drill

Drill 10: Dual-Anchor Stabilization Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in side control top.
2. Establish head control first, then hip control.
3. Hold both anchors for a 3-count before any transition attempt.
4. Transition to knee-on-belly or mount only if both anchors stay connected.
5. If either anchor is lost, reset to side control and re-anchor before continuing.

Coaching cues:

1. Keep chest pressure connected while re-anchoring hips.
2. Reward stable control windows before speed or submission attempts.
3. Require a verbal cue each rep: "head, hips, then move."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in side control top.
2. Goal: maintain both anchors before transitions or submissions.
3. Round 1 constraint: no submission attempts until a 5-second dual-anchor hold.
4. Round 2 progression: allow submissions only after a clean stabilization hold.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained control sequencing from top: head first, hips second, then movement.
If one anchor is loose, progression usually fails. If both anchors stay connected, transitions and submissions become safer and more reliable.
In live rounds this week, score yourself on clean dual-anchor holds before attacks, not on fast finishes.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Mount top: head and hip control reduces bridge success.
2. Back control: head steering plus hip control improves finish timing.
3. Passing finish: control hips before settling chest pressure.
4. Takedown finishes: head position plus hip block improves control after the mat return.

## Win Condition

Top player consistently establishes and maintains head and hip control before advancing or attacking.

## Metric

Uninterrupted dual-control segments per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean dual-anchor holds of 5 seconds per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: dual-anchor holds that lead to controlled positional advancement.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

2. From top control, check head then hips every 5 seconds.
3. If one anchor is lost, recover it before attacking.
4. Track uninterrupted dual-control windows.
5. Add one reset rule per round: if either anchor breaks during a transition, return to stabilization first.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Top player crossfaces but ignores hips.
   Quick correction: Reposition knee or hip block before attacking.
2. Mistake: Chasing submissions while anchors are loose.
   Quick correction: Re-stabilize both anchors first.
3. Mistake: Pressure is heavy but disconnected.
   Quick correction: Reconnect chest and hip lines before advancing.
4. Mistake: Student transitions immediately after touching one anchor.
   Quick correction: Require a full 3-count with both anchors before moving.
