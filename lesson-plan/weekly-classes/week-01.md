# Week 1: Positional Hierarchy And Decision Loop

## Class Goal

Students understand where they are in the positional map and can make the first correct decision before moving.

## Concepts

1. Positional hierarchy.
2. Universal decision framework (Six Questions).

## Memory Phrase

Position first, then technique.

## Whiteboard Content

### Positional Hierarchy (Worst to Best)

1. Tier 1 Defensive: back controlled bottom, mount bottom, side control bottom.
2. Tier 2 Recovery: turtle, half guard bottom, transitional frames.
3. Tier 3 Neutral/Guard: closed guard, open guard.
4. Tier 4 Offensive: passing positions, top half guard.
5. Tier 5 Dominant: side control top, knee-on-belly, mount top, back control top.
6. Tier 6 Submission Phase: strangles and joint locks.

Rule to write in big letters: advance one tier at a time.

### The Six Questions

1. Am I safe?
2. Who has inside position?
3. Who controls head and hips?
4. Who has mobility?
5. What is the next best position?
6. What threats exist right now?

### First Three Priorities By Tier

1. Tier 1 Defensive (back controlled bottom, mount bottom, side control bottom):
   1. Get safe first: protect neck and isolated limbs.
   2. Build structure: align posture and create frames.
   3. Escape to Tier 2: recover turtle, half guard, or transitional frames.
2. Tier 2 Recovery (turtle, half guard bottom, transitional frames):
   1. Stabilize structure: base, posture, and inside connection.
   2. Reclaim inside position: hand fight and elbow-knee connection.
   3. Recover to Tier 3: return to functional guard.
3. Tier 3 Neutral/Guard (closed guard, open guard):
   1. Establish control: distance, grips, and inside leg position.
   2. Create reaction: off-balance or posture break.
   3. Advance one tier: sweep to top or force entry to passing phase.
4. Tier 4 Offensive (passing positions, top half guard):
   1. Control first: deny easy re-guard and secure inside lanes.
   2. Progress in order: beat feet, knees, hips, then upper body frames.
   3. Arrive with control: establish Tier 5 dominant position.
5. Tier 5 Dominant (side control top, knee-on-belly, mount top, back control top):
   1. Stabilize dominance: control head and hips, keep connection.
   2. Isolate a target: expose neck or limb while preventing escapes.
   3. Advance to Tier 6: submit only when control is stable.
6. Tier 6 Submission Phase (strangles and joint locks):
   1. Complete phases in order: isolate and expose before finishing.
   2. Keep control during finish: break defenses without losing position.
   3. If finish fails, reset to Tier 5 control and re-attack.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

### Hierarchy Script

If you are in Tier 1, your job is survival and escape, not submissions. If you are in Tier 3, you can attack, but still with structure. If you are in Tier 5, control first and then finish.

### Six Questions Script

Run this in order every time. If Question 1 is no, stop attacking. If you are not safe, fix safety first. Then move through the rest of the list.

## Drill

Drill 3: Positional Awareness Drill.

Setup:

1. Pair students.
2. Top partner places bottom partner in random positions.
3. Bottom partner must identify tier and first three priorities before moving.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Use this same decision order in every position.
2. From bottom side control, ask safety first before escaping.
3. From top mount, ask threats and next best position before submission hunting.
