# Week 1: Position First, Then Technique

## Class Goal

Students understand where they are in the positional map and can make the first correct decision before moving.

## Concepts

1. Positional hierarchy.
2. Universal decision framework (Six Questions).

## Memory Phrase

If I know where I am, I know what to do.

## Whiteboard Content

### Positional Hierarchy (Worst to Best)

1. Tier 1 Defensive: back controlled bottom, mount bottom, side control bottom.
2. Tier 2 Recovery: turtle, half guard bottom, transitional frames.
3. Tier 3 Neutral/Guard: closed guard, open guard.
4. Tier 4 Offensive: passing positions, top half guard.
5. Tier 5 Dominant: side control top, knee-on-belly, mount top, back control top.
6. Tier 6 Submission Phase: strangles and joint locks.

Rule to write in big letters: advance one tier at a time.

### The Six Questions

1. Am I safe?
2. Who has inside position?
3. Who controls head and hips?
4. Who has mobility?
5. What is the next best position?
6. What threats exist right now?

### First Three Priorities By Tier

1. Tier 1 Defensive (back controlled bottom, mount bottom, side control bottom):
   1. Get safe first: protect neck and isolated limbs.
   2. Build structure: align posture and create frames.
   3. Escape to Tier 2: recover turtle, half guard, or transitional frames.
2. Tier 2 Recovery (turtle, half guard bottom, transitional frames):
   1. Stabilize structure: base, posture, and inside connection.
   2. Reclaim inside position: hand fight and elbow-knee connection.
   3. Recover to Tier 3: return to functional guard.
3. Tier 3 Neutral/Guard (closed guard, open guard):
   1. Establish control: distance, grips, and inside leg position.
   2. Create reaction: off-balance or posture break.
   3. Advance one tier: sweep to top or force entry to passing phase.
4. Tier 4 Offensive (passing positions, top half guard):
   1. Control first: deny easy re-guard and secure inside lanes.
   2. Progress in order: beat feet, knees, hips, then upper body frames.
   3. Arrive with control: establish Tier 5 dominant position.
5. Tier 5 Dominant (side control top, knee-on-belly, mount top, back control top):
   1. Stabilize dominance: control head and hips, keep connection.
   2. Isolate a target: expose neck or limb while preventing escapes.
   3. Advance to Tier 6: submit only when control is stable.
6. Tier 6 Submission Phase (strangles and joint locks):
   1. Complete phases in order: isolate and expose before finishing.
   2. Keep control during finish: break defenses without losing position.
   3. If finish fails, reset to Tier 5 control and re-attack.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Today is not about winning scrambles. Today is about making correct decisions. First identify your tier, then choose the right next action.

### Hierarchy Script (0:12-0:16)

If you are in Tier 1, your job is survival and escape, not submissions. If you are in Tier 3, you can attack, but still with structure. If you are in Tier 5, control first and then finish.

### Six Questions Script (0:16-0:22)

Run this in order every time. If Question 1 is no, stop attacking. If you are not safe, fix safety first. Then move through the rest of the list.

## 60-Minute Run of Show

1. 0:00-0:05 Intro and memory phrase.
2. 0:05-0:12 Warm-up: bridge, hip switch, technical stand-up.
3. 0:12-0:22 Teach hierarchy and Six Questions.
4. 0:22-0:35 Drill 3: Positional Awareness Drill.
5. 0:35-0:43 Game: Random Position Decision Race.
6. 0:43-0:51 Positional rounds from side control bottom.
7. 0:51-1:00 Recap and weekly practice drill.

## Drill

Drill 3: Positional Awareness Drill.

Setup:

1. Pair students.
2. Top partner places bottom partner in random positions.
3. Bottom partner must say tier and first three priorities before moving.

Coaching cues:

1. Answer in under 3 seconds.
2. Correct priority matters more than speed.
3. If answer is wrong, reset and repeat.

## Game

Random Position Decision Race.

Rules:

1. Coach calls a position.
2. Student says tier and immediate priority.
3. One point for correct tier and one point for correct priority.
4. First to 10 points wins.

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in side control bottom.
2. Bottom student must say Question 1 to Question 3 out loud before escape attempt.
3. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch partners each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording (0:51-1:00)

Today we learned that good jiu-jitsu starts with location and decision, not random movement. Keep using the tier map and Six Questions in order.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Use this same decision order in every position.
2. From bottom side control, ask safety first before escaping.
3. From top mount, ask threats and next best position before submission hunting.

## Win Condition

Student identifies tier and immediate priority correctly in under 3 seconds.

## Metric

10 random prompts: correct tier plus correct priority score per student.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Tier Callout Start.
2. In every live round, spend the first 10 seconds identifying your current tier.
3. Immediately choose and attempt one tier-appropriate priority action.
4. After each round, ask: did I follow the right first priority?

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Student rushes before structure.
   Quick correction: "Freeze. Build structure first, then move."
2. Mistake: Student skips the decision order.
   Quick correction: "Return to Question 1 and run the first three questions before acting."
3. Mistake: Student chooses attack from the wrong tier.
   Quick correction: "Pick a one-tier improvement action, then restart."
4. Mistake: Student gives correct tier but wrong priority.
   Quick correction: "State safety priority first, then continue sequence."
5. Mistake: Student forgets class theme under pressure.
   Quick correction: "Say the memory phrase out loud, then reset."
