# Week 12: Choose Pressure Or Movement

## Class Goal

Students choose pressure or movement passing based on clear progress cues while preserving control.

## Concepts

1. Pressure passing.
2. Movement passing.

## Memory Phrase

Be heavy when needed, be fast when needed.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: when progress stalls, switch style once without losing connection.
2. Pressure passing: connected weight and pinning control that limits movement before advancing.
3. Movement passing: angle and mobility-based passing that redirects around frames and hooks.
4. Switch trigger: no forward progress for 3 seconds or repeated block at current lane.
5. Sequence order: identify stall, choose switch direction, keep one control point, continue pass.
6. Common fail point: students disengage fully to switch styles.
7. Control target: maintain at least one anchor through every switch.
8. Transition from Week 11: Week 11 emphasized passing gates in order, Week 12 adds tactical style switching when a gate stalls.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Style choice is tactical, not random.
Teaching point: If progress stalls, switch style once without disconnecting.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we passed with clear gate progression and frame clearing. This week we add tactical flexibility by switching between pressure and movement passing.

Today's focus is smart switching, not constant switching. Pressure and movement are tools. You choose the right one based on what your partner gives you.
Repeat with me: be heavy when needed, be fast when needed. In every rep today, switch only when progress stalls and keep control through the change.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is pressure passing. Use shoulder and hip connection to pin movement and narrow escape options.

Second concept is movement passing. Use angle changes and redirection when pressure lane is blocked or frames are too active.

Watch this rep: I begin with pressure pass and pin hips. Progress stalls for a 3-count. I keep one control point, switch angle, and move to a mobility-based finish without full disengagement.

Common mistake: student switches by backing out completely. Result: guard resets and progress is lost. Correction: keep one anchor and switch from connected contact.

Partner cue for this block: call out "switch on stall" before changing styles.

## Drill

Drill 16: Connected Style-Switch Passing Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in open guard passing scenario.
2. Begin with chosen style, pressure or movement.
3. If progress stalls for 3 seconds, switch style once while keeping one control point.
4. Continue through gate progression after switch.
5. Finish in stable control with a 3-count hold.
6. If full disengagement occurs, reset to starting gate and repeat.

Coaching cues:

1. Require explicit stall recognition before switching.
2. Reward connected switches before speed.
3. Require verbal callout each rep: "stalled, switch, connected."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in open guard passing scenarios.
2. Goal: one clean style switch when progress stalls.
3. Round 1 constraint: switch allowed only after a true 3-second stall.
4. Round 2 progression: no verbal callouts, coach evaluates whether switch timing was justified.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained tactical style switching with control integrity.
You switched because progress stalled, not because of panic, and you kept connection while changing styles.
In live rounds this week, score clean connected switches that lead to pass completion.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Top control: alternate heavy pressure and mobile transitions by moment.
2. Guard: alternate sticky control and dynamic angle creation.
3. Scramble choices: decide between stabilizing or moving quickly.
4. Takedown entries: switch from drive pressure to angle finish when initial lane stalls.

## Win Condition

Passer switches style without losing control contact.

## Metric

Successful connected style switches that improve position per round.
Target: 2 to 3 justified, connected switches per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: percentage of switches that lead to pass completion.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

3. Keep at least one control point during switch.
4. Track switches that improve position.
5. Add one quality rule per round: no switch unless you can identify the exact stall cue.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Switch happens after full disengagement.
   Quick correction: Keep one control connection during transition.
2. Mistake: Stays on failing style too long.
   Quick correction: Use 3-second stall cue to trigger switch.
3. Mistake: Switches without directional plan.
   Quick correction: Call intended target side before switching.
4. Mistake: Student switches styles without real stall.
   Quick correction: Continue current style until a clear stall cue appears.
