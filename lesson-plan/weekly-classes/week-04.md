# Week 4: Move With Purpose

## Class Goal

Students connect movement to structure and sequence, not speed.

## Concepts

1. Movement fundamentals.
2. Elbow-knee connection.

## Memory Phrase

Frame, then move.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: frame, connect elbow-knee, then recover.
2. Movement fundamentals: efficient hip and torso movement used to change position without losing structure.
3. Elbow-knee connection: bringing elbow and knee together to close space and rebuild guard structure.
4. Sequence order: frame, connect elbow-knee, then recover.
5. Common fail point: moving hips before frames are stable.
6. Recovery target: reach a functional guard position, not just temporary space.
7. Movement support rule: small bridge or shrimp can create frame-entry space, but repositioning happens after frames are set.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Today is about sequence discipline under pressure.
Teaching point: Movement works when it comes after structure. Frame first, then elbow-knee connection, then recovery.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we learned to use bone-supported frames and distance awareness to create usable space. This week we connect that space to movement in the correct sequence.

Today's focus is movement with structure. You are not trying to explode out from bad positions. You are trying to move in the correct order: frame first, then elbow-knee connection, then guard recovery. Repeat with me: frame, then move.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is movement fundamentals. Movement should solve a problem, not create a scramble. In bottom side control or mount defense, your hips move after your frames are in place, not before.

General rule for this week: small bridges, shrimping, and hip escapes can be used to create just enough space to insert frames. Repositioning movement happens after proper frames are established.

Second concept is elbow-knee connection. This is the bridge between defense and guard recovery. When your elbow and knee connect, you close the gap the top player wants to use to maintain pressure.

Watch this rep: I build frame one, frame two, then I connect elbow to knee, then I move my hips to recover guard. I do not skip the connection step.

Common mistake: student frames, then tries a big hip escape without elbow-knee connection. Result: they make space but cannot hold it. Correction: connect elbow-knee first, then move with smaller controlled hip motion.

Partner cue for this block: call out "frame, connect, recover" on every rep until sequence becomes automatic.

## Drill

Drill 6: Constraint Drill (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start from side control or mount bottom.
2. Enforce sequence order on every attempt.
3. If order breaks, reset immediately.
4. Require verbal callout each rep: "frame, connect, recover."

Coaching cues:

1. Frames must be stable before hips move.
2. Elbow-knee connection must appear before guard recovery.
3. Reward sequence quality over escape speed.

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in mount bottom or side control bottom.
2. Goal: complete the full sequence, frame, elbow-knee connection, then guard recovery.
3. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained sequence discipline under pressure: frame first, connect elbow to knee, then recover guard.
If you move before frames are stable, your escape usually collapses. If you follow the sequence, your escapes stay reliable and repeatable.
In live rounds this week, judge success by clean sequence completions, not speed.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Guard recovery: frame before shrimp to avoid flattening.
2. Top transitions: stabilize connection before advancing position.
3. Scrambles: re-establish structure before explosive movement.

## Win Condition

Student completes frame to elbow-knee sequence in correct order.

## Metric

Correct sequence completions per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean sequence completions per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: sequence completions that end in functional guard recovery.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Sequence Discipline.
2. During live rounds, require frame, elbow-knee connection, then movement on every defensive exchange.
3. If you move early, pause and restart from correct step.
4. Track clean sequence completions each round.
5. Add one elbow-knee check per round before every guard recovery attempt.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Student skips frame phase.
   Quick correction: Return to first missing step before continuing.
2. Mistake: Elbow and knee stay disconnected.
   Quick correction: Bring elbow to knee before trying to recover guard.
3. Mistake: Speed increases while sequence quality drops.
   Quick correction: Reduce pace by 30 percent and prioritize order.
4. Mistake: Student stays static waiting for perfect frames and never creates entry space.
   Quick correction: Use a small bridge or shrimp to create frame-entry space, then set frames before bigger movement.
