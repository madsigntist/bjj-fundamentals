# Week 2: Base And Posture

## Class Goal

Students can hold base and posture under moderate pressure before attempting movement.

## Concepts

1. Base.
2. Posture.

## Memory Phrase

Strong base, strong choices.

## Whiteboard Content

### Base For Beginners

1. Build a wide, stable platform with knees apart and feet engaged on the mat.
2. Keep weight centered, not leaning too far forward or backward; imagine your centered weight sits just under your jaw.
3. Keep hips low enough to stay stable, high enough to move when needed.

### Posture For Beginners

1. Head up, chest up, spine aligned.
2. Elbows in and close or connected to the body.
3. Hips under shoulders. Do not fold at the waist.

### Close Guard Top: Base And Posture Checklist

1. Knees wide for balance.
2. Head over hips, eyes forward.
3. Elbows tight to ribs. No extended arms, and do not cross your arms over your opponent's centerline.
4. Hands placed with purpose, one for frame or post and one for control; do not post on the ground, post on your opponent.
5. If posture breaks, reset posture before trying to open guard.

Why?

1. Stable base distributes force so you do not burn energy fighting gravity or resisting off-balance pulls.
2. Aligned posture means attacks hit your skeletal structure instead of collapsing weak points.
3. Good base makes decisions faster because you are not recovering balance before you can act.
4. Posture keeps your arms and neck safe by default, reducing the number of threats you need to actively defend.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Today we build the structure that keeps you safe under pressure.
Teaching point: If your base and posture collapse, your options disappear.

### Base Script

For beginners, think of base as your anti-fall system. Knees wide, weight centered, and no overreaching. If your partner bumps or pulls and you stay stable, your base is working.

### Posture Script

Posture means your spine is aligned and your elbows are safe. Head up, chest up, elbows in. If your posture breaks in closed guard, reset posture first before you do anything else.

## Drill

Close Guard Top Base and Posture Ladder.

Setup:

1. Start in closed guard with top player focused only on base and posture.
2. Bottom partner gives controlled pulls, bumps, and collar-break attempts at 30, 50, then 70 percent.
3. Top player must recover to checklist posture each time before progressing.
4. Run in 60-second rounds, then switch.

## Drill 2

Mount Top Posture Recovery.

Setup:

1. Start in mount top.
2. Bottom partner gives controlled bridges and bump attempts at 30, 50, then 70 percent.
3. Top player recovers head-over-hips posture and elbows-in alignment after each disruption.
4. Run in 60-second rounds, then switch.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Posture applies in every major position, not just closed guard top.
2. Mount top: keep head over hips and elbows safe so bridges do not break your structure.
3. Side control top: maintain long spine and connected shoulders so pressure stays stable.
4. Bottom side control: align spine and head position before trying to move.
5. Standing engagements: head over hips and neutral spine improve balance during grip fighting.
