# Week 2: Structure Keeps You Safe

## Class Goal

Students can hold base and posture under moderate pressure before attempting movement.

## Concepts

1. Base.
2. Posture.

## Memory Phrase

Strong base, strong choices.

## Whiteboard Content

### Base For Beginners

1. Build a wide, stable platform with knees apart.
2. Keep weight centered, not leaning too far forward or backward.
3. Keep hips low enough to stay stable, high enough to move when needed.

### Posture For Beginners

1. Head up, chest up, spine aligned.
2. Elbows in and connected to the body.
3. Hips under shoulders. Do not fold at the waist.

### Close Guard Top: Base And Posture Checklist

1. Knees wide for balance.
2. Head over hips, eyes forward.
3. Elbows tight to ribs. No extended arms.
4. Hands placed with purpose, one for frame or post and one for control.
5. If posture breaks, reset posture before trying to open guard.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Today we build the structure that keeps you safe under pressure.
Teaching point: If your base and posture collapse, your options disappear.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we mapped positional hierarchy and decision priorities. This week we build the base and posture that make those decisions possible under pressure.

Today is about structure, not speed. If your structure is good, you stay safe and can make better decisions.

### Base Script (0:12-0:16)

For beginners, think of base as your anti-fall system. Knees wide, weight centered, and no overreaching. If your partner bumps or pulls and you stay stable, your base is working.

### Posture Script (0:16-0:22)

Posture means your spine is aligned and your elbows are safe. Head up, chest up, elbows in. If your posture breaks in closed guard, reset posture first before you do anything else.

## Drill

Close Guard Top Base and Posture Ladder.

Setup:

1. Start in closed guard with top player focused only on base and posture.
2. Bottom partner gives controlled pulls, bumps, and collar-break attempts at 30, 50, then 70 percent.
3. Top player must recover to checklist posture each time before progressing.
4. Run in 60-second rounds, then switch.

Coaching cues:

1. Say "head up, elbows in" every rep.
2. If posture breaks, pause and rebuild before continuing.
3. Reward clean recovery, not force.

## Game

Break and Recover Posture Race.

Rules:

1. Start in closed guard with top player trying to keep base and posture.
2. Bottom player has 20 seconds to break posture using legal beginner grips and pulls.
3. Top player scores one point each time they recover posture in under 3 seconds.
4. Bottom player scores one point each successful posture break held for 3 seconds.
5. Switch every minute.

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in closed guard with top player focusing on base and posture only.
2. Round 1 goal: top player maintains posture for full round.
3. Round 2 goal: top player recovers posture after each break attempt.
4. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained the two habits that keep beginners safe: stable base and recoverable posture. If posture breaks, reset first.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Posture applies in every major position, not just closed guard top.
2. Mount top: keep head over hips and elbows safe so bridges do not break your structure.
3. Side control top: maintain long spine and connected shoulders so pressure stays stable.
4. Bottom side control: align spine and head position before trying to move.
5. Standing engagements: head over hips and neutral spine improve balance during grip fighting.

## Win Condition

Student recovers closed-guard-top posture within 3 seconds after a break attempt.

## Metric

Successful posture recoveries in under 3 seconds per student.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Posture Reset Rule.
2. During live rounds, whenever posture breaks in closed guard top, stop forcing actions and reset posture first.
3. Use cue words: head up, elbows in, hips under shoulders.
4. Track clean posture recoveries before guard opens or position changes.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Top player puts hands too far forward and gets pulled down.
   Quick correction: "Hands back to safe posts. Head over hips, not over partner's chest."
2. Mistake: Top player elbows flare and arms get isolated.
   Quick correction: "Pin elbows to ribs. Short arms, strong frame."
3. Mistake: Top player tries to open guard while posture is broken.
   Quick correction: "Stop. Rebuild posture first, then restart the open sequence."
4. Mistake: Top player leans backward too far and loses balance.
   Quick correction: "Return to centered base. Shoulders over hips, knees wide."
5. Mistake: Student panics when pulled forward.
   Quick correction: "One breath, posture checklist: head up, chest up, elbows in."
