# Week 9: Guard Retention In Layers

## Class Goal

Students recover guard one layer at a time, then re-enter offense from a stable Layer 1 guard.

## Concepts

1. Guard retention layers.
2. Inside leg positioning.

## Memory Phrase

When one layer breaks, build the next.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: when a layer breaks, rebuild the next available layer before attacking.
2. Layer 3: emergency defense when passing pressure is high and guard structure is mostly broken.
3. Layer 2: partial guard structure with one meaningful frame or leg barrier re-established.
4. Layer 1: functional guard where you can defend, move, and begin offense safely.
5. Inside leg positioning: keep knee and shin inside lane to block chest-to-hip connection.
6. Common fail point: students attack from Layer 2 or Layer 3 before full recovery.
7. Recovery rule: if passer clears your inside leg, return to framing and rebuild nearest layer first.
8. Transition from Week 8: Week 8 built top control-first decision making, Week 9 teaches bottom recovery against that same pressure.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Layer thinking prevents panic and improves retention decisions.
Teaching point: Recover one layer at a time. Do not force offense until Layer 1 returns.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we emphasized control before attack from top positions. This week we apply that same discipline to guard retention in layers.

Today's focus is calm recovery under pressure. If one layer fails, you do not panic. You rebuild the next available layer and keep climbing.
Repeat with me: when one layer breaks, build the next. In every rep today, no offense until Layer 1 is fully restored.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is guard retention layers. Think in order: Layer 3 emergency survival, Layer 2 partial structure, Layer 1 functional guard. Your job is to climb layers, not skip them.

Second concept is inside leg positioning. Your inside knee and shin are key barriers that prevent passes. If your inside leg is outside and disconnected, retention usually fails.

Watch this rep: passer clears one frame, I recover Layer 3 posture, then rebuild Layer 2 with frame and inside leg, then restore Layer 1 functional guard. Only then do I begin offense.

Common mistake: student recovers partial guard and immediately attacks. Result: passer re-clears and passes. Correction: confirm Layer 1 first, then attack.

Partner cue for this block: call out your current layer each rep, then say "climb" before the next recovery step.

## Drill

Drill 13: Layer Climb Retention Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in near-pass scenario with guard player at Layer 3.
2. Guard player rebuilds to Layer 2 using frames and inside leg re-entry.
3. Guard player climbs to Layer 1 functional guard before any attack.
4. Add one controlled offense only after Layer 1 is confirmed.
5. If passer clears inside leg or collapses frames, reset to broken layer and rebuild.
6. Increase resistance in stages after two clean Layer 3-to-1 climbs.

Coaching cues:

1. Require layer callout every rep: "Layer 3, Layer 2, Layer 1."
2. Reward clean layer sequence before speed.
3. Require verbal cue before offense: "Layer 1 first."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in near-pass scenarios.
2. Goal: recover to Layer 1 before attack attempts.
3. Round 1 constraint: guard player must call out first broken layer after each failed rep.
4. Round 2 progression: no coach reset help, player self-corrects and climbs layers live.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained layer-by-layer retention under pressure, not panic reactions.
When one layer broke, you rebuilt the next layer and kept climbing until Layer 1 was restored.
In live rounds this week, score clean Layer 3-to-1 recoveries before attempting offense.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Half guard defense: same layer mindset improves stability.
2. Scramble defense: recover one layer of safety before attacking.
3. Passing defense: rebuild structure in steps rather than one explosive move.
4. Turtle recovery: rebuild frames and inside connection in layers before standing up.

## Win Condition

Guard player recovers from Layer 3 to Layer 1 before attacking.

## Metric

Layer 3-to-1 recoveries completed before offense per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean Layer 3-to-1 climbs per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: percentage of failed reps where first broken layer is correctly identified.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Student attempts sweep from broken layer.
   Quick correction: Rebuild next layer first.
2. Mistake: Legs outside and disconnected.
   Quick correction: Reinsert inside leg position before grips.
3. Mistake: Panic bridging without frames.
   Quick correction: Re-frame, then recover layer.
4. Mistake: Student declares Layer 1 without functional control and gets re-passed.
   Quick correction: Confirm frame plus inside leg plus posture before calling Layer 1.
