# Week 7: Escape Chain Integration

## Class Goal

Students complete full escape sequence to functional guard under resistance.

## Concepts

1. Full escape sequence under pressure.
2. Recovery to functional guard.

## Memory Phrase

Survive, align, frame, recover.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: fix the first broken link in your escape chain before trying the next step.
2. Escape chain under pressure: survive, align, frame, then recover guard in order.
3. Functional guard recovery: reaching a guard where you can defend, move, and re-attack safely.
4. Sequence standard: do not skip to movement before survival and structure are stable.
5. Common fail point: students create space but stop before re-establishing functional guard.
6. Recovery target: complete the chain to functional guard, not just temporary space.
7. Correction rule: after failed reps, identify first failed step and replay that step before full retry.
8. Transition from Week 6: head and hip control taught top stability, this week teaches bottom recovery against stable top pressure.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: A partial escape is not complete. Functional guard is the finish line.
Teaching point: When sequence breaks, identify the first broken link and fix that step first.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we trained head-and-hip control to limit escapes. This week we integrate the full escape chain so defense turns into functional guard recovery.

Today's focus is finishing escapes with discipline. Space alone is not the win. Functional guard is the win.
Repeat with me: survive, align, frame, recover. In every rep today, if the chain breaks, find the first broken link and repair that before continuing.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is full escape sequencing under pressure. The order matters: survive first, align your body, build frames, then recover to functional guard.

Second concept is recovery standard. You are not done when you create a little space. You are done when you return to a guard where you can defend and move safely.

Watch this rep: I stabilize safety first, align my hips and shoulders, build two functional frames, then recover to guard. I do not skip steps when I feel pressure.

Common mistake: student creates space and immediately explodes without structure. Result: top player re-pins. Correction: identify first broken link, rebuild it, then continue chain.

Partner cue for this block: call out "first broken link" after every failed rep before restarting.

## Drill

Drill 11: First-Broken-Link Escape Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in side control or mount bottom.
2. Run the full sequence: survive, align, frame, recover.
3. If the rep fails, identify first failed step immediately.
4. Replay only that failed step once with focus.
5. Restart full sequence attempt under the same pressure.
6. Increase resistance in stages after clean sequence completion.

Coaching cues:

1. Stop reps at first sequence break, do not let bad chains continue.
2. Reward clean order and complete guard recovery before speed.
3. Require verbal callout each rep: "first broken link."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in side control bottom or mount bottom.
2. Goal: reach functional guard with sequence discipline.
3. Round 1 constraint: call out first broken link after every failed attempt.
4. Round 2 progression: no reset help, student self-corrects sequence live.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained complete escapes, not partial wins. The chain only counts when it ends in functional guard.
When your sequence failed, you did not guess. You identified the first broken link, fixed it, and rebuilt the chain.
In live rounds this week, track clean chain completions before speed or scramble reactions.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Back defense: same sequence logic, safety then structure then recovery.
2. Turtle transitions: align and frame before movement.
3. Scrambles: return to sequence when chaos rises.
4. Guard retention: if retention fails, identify first broken link and repair that step first.

## Win Condition

Student consistently reaches functional guard through correct sequence order under resistance.

## Metric

Escape chain completion rate to functional guard per round.
Target: 2 to 3 clean chain completions per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: percentage of failed reps where first broken link is correctly identified.

4. Track your most common failed step this week.
5. Add one quality rule per round: no rep counts unless it finishes in functional guard.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Student skips survival and attacks too early.
   Quick correction: Return to Question 1, safety first.
2. Mistake: Frames collapse during movement.
   Quick correction: Rebuild frames before next motion.
3. Mistake: Stops after space instead of guard recovery.
   Quick correction: Continue sequence until functional guard is established.
4. Mistake: Student names the wrong broken link and repeats the wrong fix.
   Quick correction: Slow down and identify the first step that failed in sequence order.
