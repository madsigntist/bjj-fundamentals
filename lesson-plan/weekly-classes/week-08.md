# Week 8: Control Before Attack

## Class Goal

Students establish stable top control, then transition or attack without losing connection.

## Concepts

1. Pinning mechanics.
2. Connection and pressure.

## Memory Phrase

Hold first, attack second.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: hold stable control before every transition or attack.
2. Pinning mechanics: body alignment and anchor points that prevent escape movement.
3. Connection and pressure: continuous chest, head, and hip connection that stays active during transitions.
4. Sequence order: settle control, verify anchors, then move or attack.
5. Common fail point: students transition quickly after temporary control.
6. Control target: maintain connection through position changes, not just static holds.
7. Recovery rule: if connection breaks, return to control and re-anchor before attacking.
8. Transition from Week 7: Week 7 finished escapes to functional guard, Week 8 builds top control that prevents those recoveries.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Submission opportunities grow from stable control, not rushed movement.
Teaching point: Keep connection through transitions. If connection breaks, reset control first.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we built escape-chain discipline under pressure. This week we shift to top control and learn to stabilize before attacking.

Today's focus is control quality, not attack speed. If control is unstable, your transitions and submissions will fail.
Repeat with me: hold first, attack second. In every rep today, confirm control first, then transition or attack.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is pinning mechanics. In top side control, your chest angle, shoulder line, and hip position must limit your partner's ability to turn and recover guard.

Second concept is connection and pressure. Pressure is not just heavy weight. Pressure is connected weight that follows movement and keeps anchors during transitions.

Watch this rep: I settle side control, verify head and hip connection, hold for a 3-count, then transition to mount while keeping contact. I do not move before control is stable.

Common mistake: student jumps to attack after a loose pin. Result: opponent escapes during transition. Correction: re-set control, hold the 3-count, then move.

Partner cue for this block: call out "hold, then move" before each transition.

## Drill

Drill 12: Control-to-Transition Integrity Chain (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start in side control top.
2. Establish stable control and hold a 3-count.
3. Transition side control to mount while keeping chest-head-hip connection.
4. Hold mount for a 3-count, then transition back to side control with no disconnect.
5. Add controlled attack entry only after two clean transition cycles.
6. If connection breaks at any point, reset to side control and restart count.

Coaching cues:

1. Keep at least two anchors connected during every transition.
2. Reward control integrity before speed.
3. Require verbal callout each rep: "hold, then move."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Start in side control top.
2. Goal: stable control before transitions or attacks.
3. Round 1 constraint: no submission attempts until a clean 5-second control hold.
4. Round 2 progression: submissions allowed only after a stable transition sequence.
5. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained control-first decision making: settle, verify anchors, then transition or attack.
When control stayed connected, your transitions stayed safe and your attacks became higher percentage.
In live rounds this week, score clean control-to-transition sequences before submission attempts.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Mount: stabilize base before isolation attempts.
2. Back control: secure seatbelt and hooks before choke setups.
3. Passing finish: settle control before submission hunting.
4. Takedown finishes: stabilize top connection before releasing the mat return.

## Win Condition

Top player keeps stable control through at least one clean transition before attacking.

## Metric

Control-to-transition sequences completed without connection breaks.
Target: 2 to 3 clean control-transition cycles per 4-minute round.

Secondary metric: attacks attempted only after verified control holds.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Control Before Attack Count.
2. From dominant positions, hold control for a count of three before attacking.
3. If control breaks, reset and restart count.
4. Track attacks attempted after stable control.
5. Add one integrity rule per round: no transition counts unless two anchors stay connected.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Transitioning too early and losing chest contact.
   Quick correction: Hold and settle pressure before moving.
2. Mistake: Chasing submission from unstable pin.
   Quick correction: Re-establish head and hip control first.
3. Mistake: Overcommits weight and loses mobility.
   Quick correction: Keep balanced base while maintaining pressure.
4. Mistake: Student counts control without verifying anchors.
   Quick correction: Confirm two anchors before starting the hold count.
