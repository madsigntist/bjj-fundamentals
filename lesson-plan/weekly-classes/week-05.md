# Week 5: Win The Inside Battle

## Class Goal

Students win inside position first, then progress safely from seated-to-butterfly and standing exchanges.

## Concepts

1. Inside position.
2. Hand fighting basics.

## Memory Phrase

Inside wins exchanges.

## Whiteboard Content

1. Decision rule under pressure: win inside position before your next escape or attack.
2. Inside position: your hands, forearms, elbows, or knees occupy the lane between your opponent's centerline and your own.
3. Hand fighting basics: pummel, strip, and re-enter inside lanes without losing posture.
4. Sequence order: hand fight to inside, stabilize posture, then improve position.
5. Common fail point: reaching from outside and exposing arms or neck.
6. Control target: keep at least one reliable inside connection while advancing position.
7. Recovery rule: if inside position is lost, pause advancement and recover inside first.
8. Application order for today: seated to butterfly first, standing transfer second.

## Suggested Teaching Wording

Opening: Inside position decides who leads the exchange.
Teaching point: Win inside first, then move. If you move from outside position, you are usually late and exposed.

### Opening Script (0:00-0:05)

Last week we trained movement order and elbow-knee connection for reliable guard recovery. This week we add inside position and hand fighting so exchanges start in our favor.

Today's focus is who controls the inside lane first. If you are inside, you can build offense or safe defense. If you are outside, you are reacting.
Repeat with me: inside wins exchanges. In every rep today, recover inside first, then improve your position.
We will train this first from seated to butterfly, then transfer the same rule to standing hand-fighting.

### Core Script (0:12-0:22)

First concept is inside position. Inside means your elbows and hands are in front of and inside your opponent's control line, not flared outside where they can dominate ties.

Second concept is hand fighting basics. Use short, efficient actions: pummel inside, strip controlling grips, and immediately re-enter inside lanes.

Watch this rep from seated: I recover one inside tie, then two, then I establish butterfly hooks and posture. Only after inside control is stable do I off-balance or advance.

Now watch the standing transfer: same sequence, recover inside ties first, stabilize posture, then enter safely.

Common mistake: student reaches wide from outside and tries to move first.
Result: they get controlled or exposed. Correction: strip or pummel first, stabilize inside elbow position, then advance.

Partner cue for this block: call out "inside first" on every rep before movement begins.

## Drill

Drill 7: Inside Recovery Chain from Seated to Butterfly (Level 2).

Setup:

1. Start from neutral seated hand-fighting.
2. Recover one inside lane with a pummel or strip.
3. Recover second inside lane and stabilize posture.
4. Establish butterfly hooks only after inside ties are stable.
5. Add one controlled off-balance or technical stand-up entry.
6. If inside control is lost at any point, reset to hand-fight phase.

Standing transfer progression (0:35-0:43):

1. Start from neutral standing hand-fighting.
2. Recover inside ties before any step-in entry.
3. Add one safe entry after inside control is stable.
4. Reset immediately if posture breaks or inside ties are lost.

Coaching cues:

1. Keep elbows tight and connected while hand fighting.
2. Reward clean inside recovery before speed.
3. Require a verbal cue each rep: "inside first."

## Positional Rounds (0:43-0:51)

1. Round 1 starts from seated-to-butterfly hand-fighting.
2. Round 2 starts from standing hand-fighting.
3. Goal in both rounds: recover inside position before advancing.
4. 2 rounds x 4 minutes, switch roles each round.

## Suggested Recap Wording

Today we trained sequence discipline for hand fighting: recover inside, stabilize, then advance.
If you move while outside, you usually get controlled. If you win inside first, you create safer and more reliable progress from seated butterfly and standing.
In live rounds this week, score yourself on clean inside recoveries before movement, not on scramble speed.

## Concept Transfer To Other Positions

1. Standing hand fight: inside ties reduce exposure to snap-downs and drags.
2. Passing: inside arm and leg lanes create safer entries.
3. Submission defense: inside hand position protects neck and limb lines.

## Win Condition

Student consistently reclaims inside position before advancing from seated-to-butterfly or standing exchanges.

## Metric

Inside recoveries leading to positional improvement in both seated and standing rounds.
Target: 2 to 3 clean inside recoveries per 4-minute round in each start position.

Secondary metric: inside recoveries that lead to safe positional advancement.

## Practice This Week In Normal Class (Personal Live-Round Drill)

1. Personal drill: Inside First.
2. In live rounds, always recover inside position before your next move from seated or standing.
3. Use one hand-fight action, then one positional improvement.
4. Track successful inside recoveries each round.
5. Add one reset rule per round: if you lose inside position, pause and recover inside before continuing.

## Common Student Mistakes And Quick Corrections

1. Mistake: Student reaches from outside and gets controlled.
   Quick correction: Pummel back inside before attacking.
2. Mistake: Hand fighting without posture.
   Quick correction: Rebuild posture, then strip grips.
3. Mistake: Wins inside briefly, then gives it back.
   Quick correction: Keep elbow position tight after recovery.
4. Mistake: Student advances position before inside control is stable.
   Quick correction: Freeze and return to inside recovery sequence before moving.
5. Mistake: Student inserts butterfly hooks before hand control is established.
   Quick correction: Recover and stabilize inside ties first, then add hooks.
