# BJJ Fundamentals Weekly Classes: Ready-to-Teach Plan (14 Weeks)

## Program Rules

1. One class per week, 60 minutes.
2. One headline theme per class.
3. Two concepts maximum per class.
4. One cooperative drill per concept.

## Class Flow

1. Whiteboard: cover theme, concepts, and key cues.
2. Drill 1: cooperative drill for first concept.
3. Setup: discuss context and setup for second drill.
4. Drill 2: cooperative drill for second concept.
5. Feedback: open discussion to share insights on the positions.

---

## Week 1: Positional Hierarchy And Decision Loop

### Concepts

1. Positional hierarchy.
2. Universal decision framework (Six Questions).

### Drill 1: Positional Awareness

Coach calls random position. Student names tier and first priority.

### Drill 2: Six Questions Application

Start in side control bottom. Verbalize first three priorities before moving.

---

## Week 2: Base And Posture

### Concepts

1. Base.
2. Posture.

### Drill 1: Base Retention

Partner applies light directional force. Bottom player maintains base only.

### Drill 2: Posture Recovery

Start in closed guard top. Recover posture after each break attempt.

---

## Week 3: Frames And Distance Management

### Concepts

1. Frames.
2. Distance management.

### Drill 1: Frame Building

Start in side control bottom. Build two functional frames before any movement.

### Drill 2: Frame Plus Movement

Frame entry followed by hip escape or bridge. Frames preserve the space movement creates.

---

## Week 4: Movement Fundamentals And Elbow-Knee Connection

### Concepts

1. Movement fundamentals.
2. Elbow-knee connection.

### Drill 1: Frame to Movement Sequence

Start in side control or mount bottom. Frame first, then move. Reset on sequence break.

### Drill 2: Elbow-Knee Connection

After frames are set, connect elbow to knee (overlap for stronger connection), then recover guard.

---

## Week 5: Inside Position And Hand Fighting

### Concepts

1. Inside position.
2. Hand fighting basics.

### Drill 1: Inside Recovery

From seated to butterfly. Recover inside ties before establishing hooks.

### Drill 2: Grip Stripping

Strip collar, sleeve, and pants grips using hook-and-unhook method, then re-enter inside lanes.

---

## Week 6: Head Control And Hip Control

### Concepts

1. Head control.
2. Hip control.

### Drill 1: Head Control

From side control top. Turn opponent's chin the direction you want them to go. Hold and steer.

### Drill 2: Hip Control

From side control top. Block and pin hip movement. Combine with head control for dual anchor.

---

## Week 7: Escape Chain Integration

### Concepts

1. Full escape sequence under pressure.
2. Recovery to functional guard.

### Drill 1: Escape Sequence

Start in side control bottom. Run full sequence: survive, align, frame, then recover to guard.

### Drill 2: Escape to Guard Recovery

After escape sequence, recover to a functional guard position. Diagnose which step fails.

---

## Week 8: Top Control Fundamentals

### Concepts

1. Pinning mechanics.
2. Connection and pressure.

### Drill 1: Pin and Pressure

From side control top. Maintain chest-to-chest connection and pin priorities.

### Drill 2: Controlled Transitions

Transition from side control to mount without losing connection or pressure.

---

## Week 9: Guard Retention Layers And Inside Leg Position

### Concepts

1. Guard retention layers.
2. Inside leg positioning.

### Drill 1: Layer Recovery

Passer starts near pass. Guard player recovers up one layer at a time.

### Drill 2: Inside Leg Positioning

Hip escape to shin insert to hook recovery. Prioritize inside leg placement.

---

## Week 10: Off-Balancing And Sweep Vs Submission Choice

### Concepts

1. Off-balancing.
2. Sweep versus submission decision.

### Drill 1: Off-Balancing

From guard. Create reactions with kuzushi pulls and angle changes.

### Drill 2: Sweep or Submit

Read partner's defensive reaction. Alternate between sweep and submission based on what opens.

---

## Week 11: Passing Gates

### Concepts

1. Passing gates.
2. Frame clearing urgency.

### Drill 1: Gate Progression

Start in open guard. Passer clears gates in order: feet, knees, hips, upper body.

### Drill 2: Frame Clearing

Once legs are passed, clear remaining frames with urgency to settle into control.

---

## Week 12: Pressure Passing Vs Movement Passing

### Concepts

1. Pressure passing.
2. Movement passing.

### Drill 1: Pressure Passing

Maintain heavy contact and slow progression through gates.

### Drill 2: Movement Passing

Use speed and angles to clear gates. Switch between pressure and movement based on opponent response.

---

## Week 13: Submission Phases

### Concepts

1. Five submission phases.
2. Mechanical efficiency.

### Drill 1: Five-Phase Submission Sequence

From mount or back. Run all five phases in order without skipping.

### Drill 2: Efficient vs Inefficient Finish

Compare clean mechanical finish with forced finish. Prioritize structure over strength.

---

## Week 14: Dilemma Creation And Chaining

### Concepts

1. Dilemma creation and chaining.
2. Pacing and energy management.

### Drill 1: Dilemma Chains

Create a two-threat dilemma and chain to the next action based on opponent response.

### Drill 2: Full System Rounds

Mini rounds applying the full system. Manage pacing and energy across exchanges.
